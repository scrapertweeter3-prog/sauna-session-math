A home sauna is a fixed cost with a session counter

Break-even math against the gym lounge

A two-person electric sauna lands around $2,500 installed: the unit, a dedicated breaker, an electrician's afternoon. Spread over five years that is $41.67 a month, sitting in the basement whether it heats or not. The recovery lounge across town charges $60 a month on the same logic, except its meter runs on attendance you keep promising.

Running it costs pocket change. A 6 kW heater through a 45-minute session pulls 4.5 kWh, about 68 cents at fifteen cents a kWh. The flat fee is the whole contest; the electricity barely moves it.

Eight sessions a month puts the rig at $5.88 a session against the lounge's $7.50. Four sessions: $11.09 against $15. The gap holds at every honest usage level, because the lounge's $60 flat fee prices the weeks you skip, and the rig's bill never asks whether you showed up. It would take about 27 sessions a month before the electricity line let the lounge win, and nobody logs 27.

The failure mode is the dead rig: five years of $41.67 for a storage shelf. Count sessions the way a protocol log counts doses, dates on the wall by the door. A quarter under twenty visits means the purchase was a lounge subscription with extra steps, and the honest number going forward is the lounge's cost per session you attended, not per month you paid.

This is the arithmetic behind the reviews on HackedSelf: protocols dosed in numbers first, cost per session before cost per month, evidence ahead of enthusiasm. A protocol you can price is a protocol you keep.
